# personaos: "Mo is the o" (motionmaxxing, from scratch)

Third version of the voiceover reel. Same paced voiceover (`../assets/vo.mp3` + `../assets/vo-edit.json`), Mo
kept, no music, the paper-white ground the user chose. The picture is new: no cards, no UI panels, no search bar.

## PAGE
- **P:** an HVAC owner pays an overseas agency thousands a month for marketing that doesn't fill the calendar.
- **A:** HVAC company owners on Reels or TikTok, often muted, wary of agencies and of "AI".
- **G:** tap the link and get started with Mo.
- **E:** from irritated (money draining) to amused and reassured (a likeable hire who just gets on with it).
- **One true thing:** Mo writes and runs the ads, and the result is booked jobs; he never stops.

## Ideas (different kinds)
1. **UI documentary on a phone** (the owner's lock screen fills with Mo's notifications, booked jobs at 2:14 AM).
   Credible, but cover the logo and it is any AI assistant's ad.
2. **One held shot of a desk** where a day passes and the work gets done. Calm, but the voiceover makes eleven
   claims in 25 s; one shot can't carry them without becoming captions.
3. **A typographic essay with a character: Mo is the o (picked).** The sentence is the picture. The agency drains
   "money" letter by letter until only its "o" is left; that "o" inflates into Mo. From then on Mo hops into
   whichever word is being said:
   - he is the "o" of "Mo" and of "growth";
   - he writes the ad copy by rolling along the line;
   - the calendar is a grid of empty o's he fills by hopping;
   - "24/7" is a ring of 24 o's lit around him;
   - he sits on "raise" until it sinks;
   - ideas stream out of him without stopping;
   - in a row of o's (the team) he is the one;
   - on "below" he hops below;
   - he lands in the "o" of "today.", and his last landing brings in the wordmark.

   **Why:** only this brand has a round mascot and a name that ends in "os". The idea comes from the mascot's shape,
   not from the category. Cover the logo and it is still Mo; swap a competitor in and the film stops working.
   **Kind:** typographic essay with a character, not a transformation chain: Mo is the carrier, but each beat is its
   own small performance of the line.

## World (one act)
- **Ground:** paper white #FAFAF7 with a fine tooth, constant (the user's choice).
- **Light:** one soft key from the top left; Mo casts a soft lavender floor shadow; type is flat ink, as print is.
- **Depth:** the sentence on the paper, Mo above it with his shadow, and the idea pills as a near plane.
- **Texture:** paper tooth, 2.5 % grain, 180° shutter blur (the only blur grammar).
- **Sound:** the voiceover, plus small physical sounds for Mo (hops, landings, a thunk on "raise") and for fills
  (chips, a ding); no music.
- **Type:** Inter. The performed word in each beat is display size (230-260 px; "Mo" at 560 px is the one spike).
  The rest of the line is small (64 px), stacked and centred, arriving at speech rhythm.
- **Screenshot-worthy hero moment (11.0 s):** Mo mid-hop over a 5 x 4 grid of o's, half of them filled lime and
  booked, his shadow on the paper.

## Gates planned
- **G1:** every performed word is 230-560 px, and captions are 64 px (0.033 H). Captions are below 0.04 H, but they
  are the voiceover's words, not proof.
- **G3:** last event is Mo leaving at about 26.9 s; the end hold is about 0.7 s with a slow recede. The final shot
  (from the wordmark's crash) is about 1.6 s.
- **G4:** Mo plus one word per frame; captions are always small.
- **House-style risks, chosen on purpose:**
  - "A dot that becomes the logo": here Mo lands ON the wordmark rather than becoming it.
  - Big words: they are never cropped by the frame.
  - Letters peeling away: once, on the money line, where the meaning is "money leaving".
