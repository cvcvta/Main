# Puzzle Piece: "Except Mine" v5 extended script

I told him about the fire drill. I showed him a video. I even played the alarm on my phone so he'd know the sound.
The next day, every kid in his class walked outside. Except mine.
Ryan's autistic. When that alarm goes off, it's not practice. It's an emergency.
Telling him what's coming doesn't show him what it looks like.
His therapist suggested a social story: a short story that walks him through a moment before it happens.
They're an evidence-based practice for autistic kids. And research shows personal works better than generic.
In one study, generic stories didn't change a thing. Stories made just for each child did.
Great idea. Except I can't draw.
Then I found Puzzle Piece's My Story Time. It's as personal as a story can get.
It starts with his people. I uploaded a photo of his dad, and one of me. Then Evan and Sam. Even Bo, our dog.
Now they all show up in his stories, looking like themselves. No photo? You can just describe them.
I even added his teacher.
Then I told it what was hard. You can pick an everyday moment, like asking for help or being wrong, or just describe it in your own words, the way you'd tell a friend.
It wrote a story starring Ryan, at his school, with his class and his teacher. I picked his favorite art style, and a world he loves.
It can read it to him in my voice. He follows along with the highlighted words, or reads it at his own pace. And every story saves to his library, so he can read it again and again.
We read it every night that week. And at the next fire drill, he walked outside with his class.
It doesn't replace his therapist. It gives him the words before he needs them.
Make it their story. See your child's first story free with Puzzle Piece.

## On-screen citations for the recut
- **"evidence-based practice":** NCAEP, Steinbrenner et al., 2020 · National Autism Center, National Standards Project, 2015.
- **"personal works better than generic" / "one study":** Edwards, McKenney, Niekra, Hupp & Everett (2021), *Research in Autism Spectrum Disorders*, 89, 101877. Personalized narratives were more effective than generic ones for 3 of 3 students. It is a small study; have the medical/legal reviewer sign off before running.
